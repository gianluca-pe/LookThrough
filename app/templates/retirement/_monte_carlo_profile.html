Annual volatility measures how widely yearly returns vary; it is not a maximum gain or loss. Equity uses an illustrative broad-market preset of 16.30%. All compound-growth rates remain your inputs.

These volatility values are illustrative presets rather than sourced estimates: this model holds equity volatility constant throughout your projection, and bond and gold risk settings are fixed illustrative values.

For the log-return calculation, equity's annual volatility is converted using your growth input (currently {{ experiment.profile[0].log_volatility|percentage_amount(2) }}% log-return volatility). Bonds and gold use fixed log-return volatility of 6% and 16%; their equivalent annual-return volatility is shown above. Cash and deposits have no simulated volatility.

Equity and bond shocks have a +0.35 correlation; gold shocks have zero correlation with either. Zero correlation does not make gold rise when shares fall. Bonds approximate intermediate-duration investment-grade exposure; cash and deposits receive the entered fixed Liquidity return, including FDs before maturity.

Years are drawn independently using correlated normal log returns. Consecutive losses can occur, but persistent crisis regimes, changing correlations and heavy tails are not explicitly modelled. Gains above 100% are possible and are not clipped. A compound-growth input is not an arithmetic-average annual return.

Inflation stays fixed. Global shares' currency effects are implicit in their USD exposure; no separate FX paths are generated. This model cannot quantify gold's protection against variable inflation or currency-debasement regimes. Deposit default, changing reinvestment rates, trading costs and withdrawal taxes are unmodelled.
